# Grading evaluation audit

The six ProsperCoded simulations contained 24 tasks and 24 linked question-bank entries. Each entry had five anchors at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100. Five entries were already marked for review: all four Financial Accountant tasks and the Operations Coordinator data interpretation task. A clear review flag does not establish that an anchor is correct.

Confirmed examples requiring review:

- Customer Support Specialist triage: the scenario and question ask for three actions, whereas the widget and guideline use five different buckets. Its 100-point anchor combines the two contracts.
- Customer Support Specialist handoff: the candidate-visible case lacks an order reference, tracking number, value and original delivery date, but its 100-point anchor invents all four. Existing grading rationales penalized candidates for omitting unavailable facts.
- Finance reconciliation: the 100-point anchor asserts a company SOX 404 dual-approval policy that is not in the candidate-visible task.
- Finance variance bridge: the 100-point anchor describes a 1.3% variance as within a 0.5% tolerance, and assumes that tolerance despite it not being supplied in the candidate-visible task.
- Operations Coordinator carrier decision: the scenario requests a justification for an option arriving 20 November despite the 19 November commitment. A sound answer needs to acknowledge the missed commitment rather than imply that option meets it.

These are findings from targeted inspection, not a full semantic certification of all 120 anchors. The original tasks and anchors were left unchanged; a new simulation requires its own review and fresh grading. Finance weights (40% problem solving, 30% judgment, 10% communication, 20% domain awareness) remain unchanged pending calibration on independently reviewed answers. The 50/50 combination of objective and written scores for mixed tasks is an initial policy choice requiring evaluation, not a measured optimum.
